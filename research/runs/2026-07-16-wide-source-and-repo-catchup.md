## Additional source families worth adding to the research playbook

1. **Discord public server profiles** (`discord.com/servers/...`): exposes server size, online count, categories such as LFG/events/local group, supported games, and official community descriptions. Example: Kingslayer's public profile shows roughly 8.9K members, LFG/events categories, weekly MTG programming, learn-to-play language, and all branch identities.
2. **Publisher organized-play location lists**: Yu-Gi-Oh!, Flesh and Blood, Lorcana, Pokémon, Bandai, Beyblade/WBO, and other publisher lists can confirm current physical operation, exact address, phone/email, organizer continuity, and event-hosting cadence when MTG surfaces are stale. Examples found for Collectors Lounge, Shadow Realm, Joyful Toad, Games For Meeple, and others.
3. **Specialist TCG directories**: KeepUp, Card Shop Hub, CardShows.io, TCG Shop Finder, Card Shops Finder and PokémonCard.info often expose Google-derived ratings/counts, supported games, current hours, review excerpts and website/social routing. Treat as secondary evidence, not canonical truth.
4. **Marketplace footprints**: TCGplayer, Whatnot, eBay and store commerce platforms can establish active operation, sales volume, product-line emphasis and address continuity. They do not prove in-person play quality. TCGplayer product-line counts were especially useful for identifying hidden MTG depth.
5. **Meetup and organizer communities**: Meetup pages can reveal recurring third-party groups hosted at stores, organizer behavior and newcomer-placement language. Example: the Edgemont RPG club at Shuffle and Cut explicitly says organizers will try to work early arrivals into a table.
6. **Niche tournament communities**: World Beyblade Organization and similar community tournament platforms expose venue activity, organizer handles, Discord routes, event timing and practical attendance instructions. This is useful evidence of space/community operations even when the format is not MTG.
7. **Venue-category platforms**: Restaurantji, Tripadvisor, Wanderlog, MenuPix and local food/venue platforms are useful for bar/cafe/cinema venues such as Requiem and Guildhall. They expose seating, crowding, hours, accessibility, parking and atmosphere, but should not be treated as MTG schedule sources.
8. **Apple Maps, Yahoo Local, MapQuest and Roadtrippers**: useful for recent Yelp-derived excerpts, closure/relocation flags, phone/address continuity and photos. Conflicting closure flags require corroboration.
9. **Local journalism and neighborhood publications**: LAist, Boyle Heights Beat, OC-focused outlets and local community reporting can reveal third-place function, financial instability, organizer identities and community history that ordinary store pages omit.
10. **Historical identity trails**: legacy store names and predecessor listings can explain rating-count discrepancies and moves. Example: The Realm Games now identifying as Shuffle and Cut Games in Brea.
